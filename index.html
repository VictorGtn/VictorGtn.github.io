AlphaSurf
ON-THE-FLY SURFACE COMPUTATIONS FOR PROTEIN REPRESENTATION LEARNING  ·  LMRL @ ICLR 2026, EXTENDED  ·  Gertner · Cazals · Mallet
Problem

Surface encoders reach state-of-the-art results, but MSMS meshes are slow and oversized. Surfaces get precomputed and stored, which rules out augmentation, molecular dynamics and large-scale training.

Insight

The α-complex already encodes the boundary of the union of balls. We read a coarse, atom-centred mesh straight from ∂|K₀| and skip triangulation. Tufting splits atoms that expose disconnected patches.

SAS = ∂|K₀|,  S = {B(xᵢ, rᵢ + rw)}
Result

27 ms and ~1.3K vertices per protein, built on the fly during training. Accuracy on par with MSMS, better on Apo and AF2, and new uses: noise augmentation, training on MD frames, 80× cheaper S3F surfaces.

Repro details

Backbone: AtomSurf setup, ProNet residue graph + DiffusionNet surface encoder with bidirectional communication; only the surface mesh changes.

PINDER-Pair: 42,220 train dimers; test holo/apo/AF2 1,835/309/1,582 (valid meshes for all methods); AUROC from the graph head; 20 workers.

MISATO: 13,765/1,595/1,612 complexes; random MD frame at train time, frame 0 at test.

Seeds: 2024, 2025, 2026 (S3F: 2024). α-complexes with CGAL 6.2.
